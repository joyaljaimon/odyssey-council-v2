## Book 16: Ulysses Reveals Himself to Telemachus
At Eumaeus’ hut in Ithaca, Telemachus arrives safely from his journey to Pylos and is greeted warmly by the swineherd. Eumaeus departs to inform Penelope of her son's return. Meanwhile, Athena transforms Odysseus back to his true, majestic self, and he reveals his identity to an astonished and weeping Telemachus. Father and son joyfully reunite and begin planning their vengeance against the arrogant suitors. They determine the immense number of men opposing them (over a hundred) and resolve to hide the palace weapons when the time is right, keeping Odysseus’ return a strict secret from Penelope and the household. In town, the suitors' ambush ship returns empty after failing to intercept Telemachus. Antinous urges the suitors to plot a new ambush and murder Telemachus immediately before he can call an assembly, but Amphinomus counsels moderation, advising them to consult the gods first. Penelope hears of the murderous plot from Medon and publicly rebukes Antinous at the palace, though the deceitful Eurymachus falsely reassures her of her son's safety. By evening, Eumaeus returns to the hut, where Athena re-disguises Odysseus as an old beggar.
People: Odysseus (disguised as a beggar, then revealed as a father plotting revenge), Telemachus (loyal son returning from Pylos, reunites with his father), Eumaeus (loyal swineherd who hosts both father and son), Athena (goddess who aids Odysseus and manages his disguises), Penelope (grieving queen who confronts the suitors), Antinous (suitor who plots Telemachus's murder), Eurymachus (deceitful suitor who falsely reassures Penelope), Amphinomus (suitor who counsels against killing Telemachus without divine sanction).
Ideas: nostos, metis, loyalty, divine intervention, deceit and recognition.
