## Book 10: Aeolus, the Laestrygones, and Circe

Odysseus recounts his journey to the island of Aeolus, who gifts him a sack containing the winds to ensure a safe voyage home. Within sight of Ithaca, Odysseus’s crew, driven by greed and suspicion, open the sack, unleashing a storm that blows them back to Aeolus. The god, now convinced Odysseus is cursed by heaven, refuses further aid. The fleet reaches the land of the Laestrygonians, giant cannibals who destroy eleven ships and devour the crews. Only Odysseus’s ship escapes. They arrive at Aeaea, home of the goddess Circe. After Odysseus kills a stag to feed his men, he sends Eurylochus to explore. Circe transforms the scouting party into swine. Protected by the herb Moly, a gift from Hermes, Odysseus confronts Circe, forces her to restore his men, and becomes her lover. After a year of luxury, the crew urges departure. Circe agrees but mandates a terrifying detour: Odysseus must travel to the underworld to consult the prophet Teiresias. As they prepare to leave, the youngest crewman, Elpenor, falls from the roof and dies, leaving his body behind as the rest set sail for the realm of Hades.

People: Aeolus (god of winds, initially helpful, later dismissive), Antiphates (Laestrygonian king, cannibal giant), Circe (goddess/enchantress, transforms men, later a guide), Hermes (messenger god, provides Moly), Eurylochus (crewman, suspicious of Circe), Elpenor (young crewman, dies accidentally).

Ideas: Temptation, Metis (cunning), Fate, Xenia (violated by Laestrygonians and Circe).
