## Book 19: Euryclea Recognises Ulysses
Odysseus and Telemachus remove the weapons from the Great Hall, aided invisibly by Athena whose golden lamp casts a wondrous light. Retiring to rest, Odysseus is left behind and is soon joined by Penelope, who comes from her chamber to interview the mysterious stranger. Cruel Melantho taunts Odysseus again, but Penelope rebukes her and seats the beggar by the hearth to question him. Odysseus, spinning a elaborate false tale about his origins in Crete and how he once hosted Odysseus on his way to Troy, tests and breaks Penelope's heart with memories. Deeply moved, Penelope orders her old nurse, Euryclea, to wash the stranger's feet. While bathing him, Euryclea recognizes a distinctive scar on his thigh—gained years ago during a boar hunt on Parnassus with his grandfather Autolycus. She nearly cries out in joy, but Odysseus fiercely grips her throat, swearing to keep her silent and loyal. Returning to the hearth, Odysseus listens as Penelope shares a prophetic dream of an eagle slaughtering her twenty pet geese, which Odysseus interprets as the impending death of the suitors. Finally, Penelope announces her contest of the twelve axes for the coming day, deciding she will marry whichever suitor can string the great bow and shoot through them, unaware that her disguised husband sits ready to destroy them all.

People: Ulysses (disguised hero, plots the slaughter of the suitors and conceals his identity), Telemachus (obedient son, helps remove the armor), Athena (goddess, provides miraculous light in the hall), Euryclea (loyal old nurse, washes Odysseus' feet and recognizes his scar), Penelope (sorrowful queen, interviews the beggar and shares her dream/contest plans), Melantho (insolent maid, taunts Odysseus), Eurynome (faithful housekeeper).

Ideas: metis, loyalty, recognition, nostos, temptation.
