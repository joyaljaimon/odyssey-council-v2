## Book 14: Ulysses in the Hut with Eumaeus

Odysseus arrives at the hut of Eumaeus, his loyal swineherd, disguised as an old beggar. Eumaeus welcomes him with traditional xenia, despite his own poverty and the burden of feeding the suitors who occupy Odysseus's palace. Over a meal of pork, Eumaeus laments the loss of his master and expresses deep skepticism regarding rumors of Odysseus's survival. Odysseus, maintaining his disguise, fabricates a complex backstory—claiming to be a Cretan veteran of the Trojan War who survived various shipwrecks and travels through Egypt and Thesprotia. He swears a solemn oath that Odysseus will return within the month to exact vengeance. Eumaeus remains unconvinced but continues to treat the stranger with kindness, honoring the protection of Zeus. As night falls, the men eat and share stories. Odysseus tests Eumaeus’s generosity by recounting a tale from the Trojan War about needing a cloak, prompting the swineherd to provide him with a warm covering for the night. Eumaeus then leaves to sleep near his herd, demonstrating his unwavering dedication to his master’s property.

People: Odysseus (disguised as a beggar, testing loyalty), Eumaeus (loyal, hospitable swineherd), Mesaulius (a servant purchased by Eumaeus).

Ideas: Xenia, Loyalty, Disguise, Fate.
