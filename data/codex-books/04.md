## Book 4: The Visit to King Menelaus

Telemachus and Pisistratus reach Lacedaemon, where King Menelaus is celebrating the double wedding of his children. Menelaus welcomes them with lavish xenia, and upon recognizing Telemachus’s resemblance to Odysseus, all weep for the lost hero. Helen joins them, drugging their wine with an Egyptian herb to banish sorrow. Menelaus recounts his own arduous return from Troy, detailing how he trapped the sea-god Proteus to learn his fate. Proteus revealed that Agamemnon was murdered by Aegisthus and that Odysseus is currently held captive by the nymph Calypso on her island. Menelaus offers Telemachus a gift, which the youth declines, preferring a piece of plate over horses unsuitable for Ithaca. Meanwhile, back in Ithaca, the suitors discover Telemachus has secretly sailed to Pylos. Enraged, they plot to ambush and murder him upon his return. Medon informs Penelope of the conspiracy, plunging her into despair. She is only comforted when Athena sends a phantom of her sister, Iphthime, to assure her that her son is under divine protection. The book concludes with the suitors lying in wait at the island of Asteris, ready to intercept Telemachus’s ship.

People: Menelaus (king of Sparta, provides news of Odysseus), Helen (queen of Sparta, provides comfort and stories), Telemachus (seeks news of his father), Pisistratus (son of Nestor, companion to Telemachus), Penelope (grieving mother, fears for her son), Antinous (suitor, leads the plot to kill Telemachus), Proteus (sea-god, reveals the fates of the Achaeans), Athena (goddess, guides and protects Telemachus).

Ideas: Xenia, Nostos, Fate, Loyalty.
