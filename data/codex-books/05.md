## Book 5: Calypso—Ulysses Reaches Scheria on a Raft

The gods convene on Olympus, where Athena pleads for the release of Odysseus, who languishes on Ogygia under the nymph Calypso’s forced affection. Zeus agrees and dispatches Hermes to command Calypso to let him go. Though she resents the gods’ jealousy of her mortal lovers, Calypso obeys. She finds Odysseus weeping on the shore and instructs him to build a raft. Odysseus, suspicious of her motives, demands an oath before he begins construction. After four days of labor, he departs, sailing for seventeen days until he sights Scheria. Poseidon, returning from Ethiopia, spots him and, enraged, summons a catastrophic storm that shatters the raft. Odysseus is saved by the sea-goddess Ino, who gives him an enchanted veil to keep him afloat. After two days of drifting, he reaches the rocky coast of Scheria. Athena calms the winds, allowing him to swim to a river mouth. Exhausted and battered, he casts Ino’s veil into the sea, crawls into a thicket of olive trees, and falls into a deep, restorative sleep provided by Athena.

People: Odysseus (suffering hero), Athena (his divine protector), Zeus (king of gods), Calypso (nymph who detains him), Hermes (divine messenger), Poseidon (his divine antagonist), Ino (sea-goddess who aids him).

Ideas: Nostos (homecoming), Divine Intervention, Fate, Xenia (in the context of Calypso's forced hospitality).
