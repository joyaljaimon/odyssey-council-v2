## Book 9: The Cyclops

Odysseus reveals his identity to King Alcinous and recounts his journey from Troy. After sacking Ismarus, his men’s refusal to leave leads to a disastrous conflict with the Cicons. Driven by storms, they reach the land of the Lotus-eaters, where Odysseus must forcibly rescue crewmen addicted to the forgetful lotus. They then arrive at the island of the Cyclopes, a lawless, uncivilized race. Odysseus takes twelve men to explore the cave of Polyphemus, a giant son of Poseidon. Trapped by a massive stone, the men are imprisoned as the monster devours them two by two. Odysseus uses his *metis*, plying the giant with potent wine and claiming his name is "Noman." When Polyphemus falls into a drunken stupor, Odysseus and his men blind the monster’s single eye with a heated olive-wood stake. The other Cyclopes, hearing Polyphemus cry that "Noman" is hurting him, depart. Odysseus escapes by clinging to the bellies of the giant’s rams. Once at sea, his hubris compels him to reveal his true name, prompting Polyphemus to pray to his father, Poseidon, for a cursed, lonely, and delayed homecoming for the hero.

People: Odysseus (narrator, clever survivor), Alcinous (Phaeacian king), Polyphemus (Cyclops, Poseidon’s son), Cicons (warriors), Lotus-eaters (passive hosts), Poseidon (god of the sea, vengeful father).

Ideas: Metis (cunning), Hubris (pride), Xenia (violated hospitality), Nostos (homecoming).
