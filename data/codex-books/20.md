## Book 20: Signs and Revelations
As the eve of the slaughter approaches in Ithaca, a sleepless Odysseus broods over the faithless maids and the overwhelming number of suitors, but Athena comforts him and grants him sleep. Meanwhile, Penelope weeps in her bed, praying to Diana for death out of grief for Odysseus. At daybreak, Odysseus prays to Jove for a sign, and the god responds with a sudden thunderclap from a clear sky, echoed by a weary miller-woman's prayer that the suitors' feast may be their last. Telemachus inspects the palace, where Eumaeus and Philoetius arrive with livestock for the feast, both proving their steadfast loyalty and receiving Odysseus's promise of his imminent return. The suitors plot against Telemachus, but an inauspicious bird omen halts them, and they proceed to dine. Instigated by Athena to keep Odysseus's anger burning, the arrogant Ctesippus hurls an ox's foot at Odysseus, narrowly missing him. Telemachus sharply rebukes the suitors for their violence. When the doomed men break into helpless, manic laughter—their meat smeared with blood and their faces streaming with tears—the prophet Theoclymenus sees the hall wrapped in a phantom shroud of darkness and ghosts crowding to the underworld, prompting him to warn them and depart the doomed house.
People: Odysseus, Athena, Penelope, Diana, Jove, Euryclea, Telemachus, Eumaeus, Melanthius, Philoetius, Ctesippus, Agelaus, Theoclymenus, Eurymachus, Piraeus
Ideas: loyalty, divine omens, retributive justice, suppressed rage
