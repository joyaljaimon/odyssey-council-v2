## Book 2: The Assembly and Departure

Telemachus convenes the first Ithacan assembly since the departure of Odysseus. He laments the ruin of his estate by the suitors who refuse to leave his home or seek Penelope’s hand properly. Antinous blames Penelope’s cunning, specifically her ruse of the shroud, for their presence. Telemachus refuses to expel his mother, invoking the gods and threatening the suitors with divine retribution. Zeus sends two eagles as an omen, which Halitherses interprets as a sign of Odysseus’s imminent return, though Eurymachus mocks the prophecy. Telemachus requests a ship to seek news of his father in Pylos and Sparta. Despite Mentor’s rebuke of the passive Ithacan elders, the assembly dissolves without support. Athena, disguised as Mentor, encourages Telemachus, promising to secure a crew and ship. While the suitors feast, Telemachus instructs his nurse, Euryclea, to prepare provisions in secret. That night, Athena casts a deep sleep over the drunken suitors, gathers a crew, and guides Telemachus to the harbor. They set sail under a fair wind provided by the goddess, beginning the young prince’s quest to restore his father’s honor and secure his own future.

People: Telemachus (seeks his father and maturity), Athena (guides and empowers Telemachus), Antinous and Eurymachus (arrogant, predatory suitors), Halitherses (prophet of doom for the suitors), Euryclea (loyal nurse).

Ideas: Nostos, Xenia (violated by the suitors), Fate, Divine Intervention.
