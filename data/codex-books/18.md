## Book 18: The Fight with Irus
<A dense summary of 150-220 words: every major event in order, who does what and why, where it happens. Use Greek names (Odysseus, Athena, Poseidon, Zeus, Hermes).>
In Ithaca, the beggar Irus insults Odysseus and challenges him to a fight outside the palace. Antinous and the suitors encourage the match, offering a prize of blood-sausages. Athena strengthens Odysseus, who bares his mighty frame, striking terror into Irus. Wishing not to reveal his identity, Odysseus delivers a restrained blow that breaks Irus's jaw and drags him to the courtyard gates. The suitors congratulate Odysseus and shower him with food. Odysseus warns the well-meaning Amphinomus of Odysseus's imminent return and bloodshed, but Amphinomus, fated by Athena to die by Telemachus's hand, stays. Athena inspires Penelope to visit the hall. Adorned with divine beauty by Athena, Penelope rebukes Telemachus for letting the stranger be abused and chastises the suitors for courting her without traditional gifts. Odysseus rejoices at her cunning. The suitors send servants for lavish gifts—robes, gold, and necklaces—which Penelope takes upstairs. When night falls, the maids light torches; Melantho insolently taunts Odysseus, who threatens her. Eurymachus mocks Odysseus and hurls a footstool, missing Odysseus and striking a cupbearer. Telemachus rebukes the brawling suitors, and Amphinomus counsels peace before they disperse to sleep.
