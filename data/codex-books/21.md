## Book 21: The Trial of the Bow
## Book 21: The Trial of the Bow
Penelope fetches Odysseus’s great bow and axes from the storehouse, announcing to the boisterous suitors in Ithaca that she will marry whoever can string the weapon and shoot an arrow through all twelve axe-heads. The suitors try in vain, led by Antinous and Eurymachus, while Leiodes warns of the disaster the bow portends. Outside the hall, Odysseus reveals his identity to the loyal herdsmen Eumaeus and Philoetius, proving himself with his old scar from Parnassus, and instructs them to lock the courtyard gates and secure the women's quarters. Returning inside, Odysseus asks for the bow over the mocking protests of Antinous and the nervous objections of the suitors. Despite their threats and jeers, Penelope insists the stranger be given a chance. Telemachus sends his mother away to her chambers, takes control of the hall, and allows Eumaeus to hand the weapon to Odysseus. The disguised hero easily strings the bow, plucks it to sweet song, and sends an arrow cleanly through every axe-head while Zeus thunders a sign of doom.
People: Penelope (queen who sets up the trial of the bow), Odysseus (disguised hero who reveals himself and strings his bow), Telemachus (defends his mother and asserts control), Eumaeus (faithful swineherd who delivers the bow), Philoetius (faithful stockman who secures the gates), Antinous (arrogant suitor leader who mocks Odysseus), Eurymachus (leading suitor who fails to string the bow), Leiodes (priest who fails and prophesies doom), Euryclea (nurse who secures the women's quarters), Melanthius (goatherd who fetches lard for the suitors).
Ideas: xenia, metis, nostos, loyalty, hubris, temptation.
