## Book 23: The Great Secret
Euryclea awakens Penelope to announce Odysseus's return and the slaughter of the suitors, but a cautious Penelope refuses to believe her, suspecting a divine trick. Coming downstairs, she sits in silence across from Odysseus, who still wears rags; Telemachus rebukes her for her coldness, but she insists on testing the stranger with private signs. Odysseus, unfazed, plots to delay local panic by having Phemius play wedding music so they can safely escape to the country. Bathed and magically enhanced by Athena into radiant splendor, Odysseus again faces his wife, testing her further by pretending they must sleep apart. Recognizing the bait, Penelope commands Euryclea to move their immovable bridal bed. This prompts Odysseus to passionately describe how he built the bed around a living olive tree stump, proving his identity. Overjoyed, Penelope weeps and embraces him, begging forgiveness for her long hesitation. Athena halts the night to prolong their reunion, during which Odysseus recounts his wanderings and future prophecy to her. At dawn, leaving the palace disguised in darkness by Athena, Odysseus, Telemachus, Eumaeus, and Philoetius depart for Laertes' farm.
People: Odysseus (slays suitors, proves identity to Penelope), Penelope (tests Odysseus with the bed secret before embracing him), Euryclea (delivers the joyful news to Penelope), Telemachus (helps secure the house and reproaches his mother), Eurynome (washes and anoints Odysseus), Athena (prolongs the night and hides the men in darkness), Eumaeus and Philoetius (arm themselves and accompany Odysseus).
Ideas: loyalty, metis, nostos, recognition.
