## Book 15: The Return of Telemachus

Athena travels to Lacedaemon to urge Telemachus to return home, warning him of the suitors' ambush in the strait and advising him to visit Eumaeus first. After receiving lavish gifts from Menelaus and Helen, Telemachus departs with Pisistratus. An eagle carrying a goose appears on the right, which Helen interprets as a sign of Odysseus’s imminent return and vengeance. Upon reaching Pylos, Telemachus avoids Nestor’s house to ensure a quick departure. He takes aboard Theoclymenus, a fugitive seer from Argos seeking protection. Meanwhile, at the swineherd’s hut, Odysseus tests Eumaeus’s loyalty by proposing to go beg at the palace, but Eumaeus wisely dissuades him, citing the suitors' violence. Eumaeus recounts his own history: he was the son of a king on the island of Syra, kidnapped as a child by Phoenician traders after his nurse was seduced. Telemachus lands in Ithaca, instructs his crew to proceed to the city, and entrusts Theoclymenus to Piraeus. A hawk carrying a dove appears on the right, which Theoclymenus interprets as a favorable omen for the house of Odysseus. Telemachus then sets off on foot toward Eumaeus’s hut.

People: Athena (guides Telemachus), Telemachus (returns home), Menelaus/Helen (provide hospitality/gifts), Theoclymenus (fugitive seer/prophet), Eumaeus (loyal swineherd), Odysseus (tests Eumaeus).

Ideas: Nostos (the drive to return home), Xenia (the duty of hosts to guests), Fate/Omens (divine signs through birds), Loyalty (Eumaeus’s steadfastness).

QUOTES:
- (15.2) "The chief men among the suitors are lying in wait for you in the Strait between Ithaca and Samos, and they mean to kill you before you can reach home."
- (15.14) "The eagle came from the mountain where it was bred and has its nest, and in like manner Ulysses, after having travelled far and suffered much, will return to take his revenge"
- (15.29) "If you go near the suitors you will be undone to a certainty, for their pride and insolence reach the very heavens."
