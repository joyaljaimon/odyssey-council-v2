## Book 24: Peace
## Book 24: Peace
Hermes guides the ghosts of the slain suitors down to Hades, where Achilles and Agamemnon converse and listen to Amphimedon recount their massacre by Odysseus. Meanwhile, Odysseus travels to his father Laertes' orchard. Disguised and initially testing his father with a false tale, Odysseus reveals his identity and proves it with his scar and the trees Laertes once gave him. As the two feast with Telemachus and Dolius' family, the grieving townspeople of Ithaca gather, led by Eupeithes, who seeks vengeance for the dead suitors. Despite Halitherses' warning that the gods sanctioned the slaughter, the armed mob marches on Laertes' farm. Athena, consulting with Zeus, receives permission to establish a lasting peace. Rejuvenated by Athena, Laertes kills Eupeithes with his spear. Just as Odysseus and his men prepare to rout the enemy, Athena intervenes with a divine cry, commanding both sides to cease bloodshed. Disguised as Mentor, Athena brokers a solemn covenant of peace between Odysseus and the people of Ithaca.
People: Hermes (guides suitors' ghosts to Hades), Achilles (hero in Hades, speaks with Agamemnon), Agamemnon (hero in Hades, hears how suitors died), Amphimedon (suitor's ghost, recounts the massacre to Agamemnon), Odysseus (slaughters suitors, visits Laertes, fights the mob), Laertes (aging father of Odysseus, works orchard, kills Eupeithes), Telemachus (helps Odysseus prepare for battle), Eumaeus & Philoetius (loyal herdsmen), Dolius & his sons (farm workers, join Odysseus' family in arms), Penelope (praised for her fidelity), Rumour (spreads news through town), Eupeithes (leads mob to avenge Antinous, killed by Laertes), Medon & Phemius (bring news of divine intervention), Halitherses (warns the mob not to fight), Mentor / Athena (guides the combatants and establishes peace), Zeus (grants Athena permission to end the strife).
Ideas: nostos, loyalty, metis, fate
