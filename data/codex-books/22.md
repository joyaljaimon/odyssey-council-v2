## Book 22: The Slaughter of the Suitors
Shedding his rags in the palace hall at Ithaca, Odysseus turns his bow upon the arrogant suitors, instantly slaying Antinous with a throat-shot. Though Eurymachus attempts to negotiate and then rallies the men to charge, Odysseus, aided by Telemachus, Eumaeus, and Philoetius, cuts them down systematically. Melanthius, the treacherous goatherd, sneaks to the storeroom for armor to supply the suitors, but Eumaeus and Philoetius catch him, stringing him up in agonizing bondage from the rafters. Athena intervenes in the guise of Mentor, deflecting the suitors' spears and finally flashing her deadly aegis, throwing the remaining men into a panicked rout like cattle chased by gadflies. Odysseus spares only the bard Phemius and the herald Medon out of mercy and Telemachus’s intercession. Once every suitor lies dead amidst the blood-slippery floor, Odysseus summons the loyal nurse Euryclea. He forces the twelve unfaithful maidservants to scrub the gore-spattered hall before leading them outside, where Telemachus executes them by hanging. Finally, they mutilate Melanthius, and Odysseus purifies the bloody halls with fire and sulfur.
People: Odysseus, Telemachus, Eumaeus, Philoetius, Antinous, Eurymachus, Amphinomus, Melanthius, Athena, Agelaus, Leiodes, Phemius, Medon, Euryclea.
Ideas: xenia, hubris, metis, retribution, pollution and purification.
