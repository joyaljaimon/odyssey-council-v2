## Book 13: Ulysses Leaves Scheria and Returns to Ithaca

The Phaeacians host a final feast for Odysseus, who eagerly awaits sunset to depart. After exchanging gifts and final farewells, he boards their ship and falls into a deep, deathlike sleep. The Phaeacian crew rows with supernatural speed, reaching Ithaca by dawn. They deposit the sleeping Odysseus and his treasures near the cave of the Naiads before departing. Enraged by the Phaeacians' assistance, Poseidon complains to Zeus, who permits him to punish them. Poseidon turns their ship into stone as it nears Scheria, fulfilling an ancient prophecy. Meanwhile, Odysseus wakes in Ithaca but fails to recognize his home due to a mist cast by Athena. He initially suspects the Phaeacians have abandoned him elsewhere. Athena appears disguised as a shepherd, eventually revealing her identity and clearing the mist. She confirms his location and advises him on how to reclaim his house from the suitors. To ensure his safety, Athena transforms Odysseus into an aged, ragged beggar. She instructs him to seek out Eumaeus, his loyal swineherd, while she travels to Sparta to summon Telemachus home.

People: Odysseus (returns home, disguised as a beggar), Athena (guides and transforms him), Poseidon (punishes the Phaeacians), Alcinous (Phaeacian king who aids Odysseus), Eumaeus (loyal swineherd).

Ideas: Nostos, Metis, Divine Intervention, Fate.
