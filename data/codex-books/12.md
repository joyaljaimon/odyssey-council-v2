## Book 12: The Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis, The Cattle of the Sun

After burying Elpenor on Aeaea, Odysseus receives final instructions from Circe. She warns him of the Sirens, the deadly Scylla and Charybdis, and the sacred cattle of the Sun. Following her advice, Odysseus plugs his crew’s ears with wax and has himself lashed to the mast to survive the Sirens' song. They then navigate the straits; while avoiding the whirlpool Charybdis, the monster Scylla snatches six men from the ship. Exhausted and starving, the crew lands on Thrinacia. Despite Odysseus’s warnings and their oaths, Eurylochus convinces the men to slaughter the Sun-god’s cattle while Odysseus sleeps. When they set sail, Zeus destroys their ship with a thunderbolt as retribution. All the men perish at sea, leaving only Odysseus. He drifts back through the straits, narrowly escaping Charybdis by clinging to a fig tree, and is eventually washed ashore on Ogygia, where Calypso rescues him. Odysseus concludes his long narrative to the Phaeacians, ending his account of his wanderings.

People: Odysseus (narrator, sole survivor), Circe (divine guide), Sirens (enchantresses), Scylla (six-headed monster), Charybdis (whirlpool), Eurylochus (mutinous crewman), Hyperion (Sun-god), Zeus (punisher), Calypso (goddess who rescues Odysseus).

Ideas: Temptation, divine retribution, the cost of leadership, fate vs. free will, the dangers of hubris.
