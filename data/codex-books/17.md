## Book 17: The Beggar at the Manor
## Book 17: The Beggar at the Manor
Telemachus returns to Ithaca, greets his weeping mother Penelope, and tells her nothing of Odysseus except that he journeyed to Pylos and Sparta. The seer Theoclymenus prophesies that Odysseus is already on the island. Meanwhile, Odysseus and Eumaeus journey to the town. Along the way, the insolent goatherd Melanthius mocks and kicks Odysseus. At the palace gates, Odysseus's old hound Argos recognizes his master and dies of joy upon the dung heap. Entering his own hall in disguise, Odysseus begs bread from the suitors. Melanthius and Antinous abuse him, and Antinous cruelly hurls a footstool at Odysseus’s shoulder, which the hero bears with steadfast silence. Moved by the outrage, Penelope summons the stranger to question him about her husband, but Odysseus prudently asks her to wait until sundown to avoid the suitors' violence. Telemachus sneezes an omen of destruction over the hall, and Eumaeus returns to his swineherding chores as evening falls.
People: Telemachus (reunites with mother, protects Odysseus), Penelope (weeps for her husband, summons the beggar), Eumaeus (loyal swineherd, guides Odysseus to town), Melanthius (insolent goatherd, abuses Odysseus), Argos (dying loyal hound), Theoclymenus (prophesies Odysseus is in Ithaca), Antinous (haughty suitor, strikes Odysseus with a stool), Ulysses/Odysseus (disguised hero, endures insults with metis).
Ideas: xenia, hubris, metis, loyalty, nostos.
